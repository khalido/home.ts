# Google Cast

Nest speakers, Nest Hubs, Chromecasts, Google TV and speaker groups, read over CastV2 on the local network. `iot_class`: **local_push** (one socket per device or group, status pushed, a PING every 5 s). The integration only reads; the writes (volume, mute, stop, announcements) are calls on the same object, never made by the integration itself, and announcements, volume and mute have run on real devices (2026-11-09 and 2026-11-10, below); `stop` and `announceAll` only on a scripted fake socket. The design and what comes next (casting a page) are in [docs/cast.md](../../../docs/cast.md).

## Built

- `cast({ resolve, connect })` keeps every Cast device and group current as one integration. `resolve()` returns what mDNS `_googlecast._tcp` found (`id`, `name` from TXT `fn`, `model` from `md`, host, port, `group`); the CLI runs `dns-sd`. `connect` is `tlsConnect` from `@khalido/home/cast/tls`. `expect` lists the config's entries, so one discovery misses shows as disconnected rather than vanishing. From a config, `resolve` is the shared two-pass discovery, done after the first pass once every entry has answered. An entry with a cached address (the address cache, `loadHome`'s `cache`) connects at once with a 1 s timeout; discovery runs only when an entry has none or a cached address fails, so a stale one still reads as `connecting` until discovery has had its say. If discovery can't find the device either, it shows as disconnected at once rather than waiting on the same address again; the backoff still retries. A cached port other than 8009 marks a group, since devices always listen on 8009.
- Per socket: `CONNECT` to `receiver-0`, receiver `GET_STATUS`, multizone `GET_STATUS` (a group's members; on a device's own port, the device alone, by its current name), then whatever the device pushes. A running app with the media namespace gets a `CONNECT` and media `GET_STATUS` on its `transportId`, as Home Assistant does. A device's `PING` is answered with `PONG`; a socket silent for three heartbeats (15 s), or without a receiver status 10 s after connecting, is closed and the detail says which.
- A dropped socket reconnects with backoff (1 s doubling to 60 s) at whatever address discovery last gave. The drop starts one shared `resolve()` at once (a group's address changes with its leader); while anything stays down or missing, that one discovery repeats at 1, 5 and 15 minutes, then hourly, as the relocator does, never once per socket. `refresh()` re-asks every open socket and nudges the discovery, which also finds devices when nothing was found at start.
- The part is `connecting` until every device has answered or failed, then `connected` while any is up; `detail` names the ones down. A payload of a known type the schema rejects is an `anomaly`, never thrown.
- State (`CastSnapshot`, by mDNS id): `form` (`speaker`, `display` or `tv`, from `castForm(model)`; none for a model it doesn't know; the address cache keeps the model), app, `idle` (no app, or Backdrop's `isIdleScreen`), `standby`, volume (unrounded), muted, `volumeControl`, player state, title, artist; groups carry `members` and `leader`; devices carry `groups`, and `playingVia` when they show a playing group's media because their own is empty (Home Assistant's model, not yet seen live).
- Names: a device's `name` in the snapshot is its own, from discovery's `fn` or its own multizone status (the same text on all six live devices, 2026-10-13), so a rename in the Google Home app shows once the device answers, even when every address came from the cache and discovery never ran; the config's name is only the placeholder until then. A group's multizone status lists its members, not itself, so a group's new name shows at the next discovery.
- `castStatus(state)`: what each device and group is doing, for a caller choosing where to play something. `state` is `idle`, `restorable` (the Default Media Receiver, which `announce` puts back), `busy` (another app, playing or paused: an announce ends it) or `offline` (not connected, or not heard from yet); with the app, title, artist, volume, `via` (the group whose media a member is playing), `standby`, and a group's `members`. A group is as free as its least free connected member.
- `castFree(state, targets)`: the targets an announcement would not disturb, as names for `announceAll`, and `skipped` with why (`busy`, `offline`, `standby`, `unknown`, `form`). With `{ forms: ["speaker", "display"] }` a TV, or a model whose form isn't known, is skipped as `form`, and a group holding one becomes its other free members. A TV in standby is skipped, since a clip may wake it. A group stays a group while every connected member is free; one playing another app is skipped whole; otherwise a group with a busy member becomes its free members one by one (they then play out of step). A device is never named beside a free group that holds it. Both functions are pure, over the snapshot, and tested on built snapshots only: what a group's members report while it plays Spotify hasn't been checked live.
- Writes, on the object `cast()` returns (`CastActions`), over the sockets it already holds, never a second connection. A target is a name (any case) or a castId; volume is 0 to 1, as the snapshot's. `setVolume`, `setMuted` (receiver `SET_VOLUME`; a group's goes to the group's socket; per device, levels and mutes each at most 30 a minute, the newest waiting one winning; one device's lane never waits on another's) and `stop` (receiver `STOP` of the running app; nothing when idle). Each request waits 10 s for its reply and a refusal (`LAUNCH_ERROR`, `LOAD_FAILED`, …) rejects, naming it.
- `announce(target, clip, { volume?, timeoutMs? })`: `clip` is `{ url, type? }` or `{ bytes, type }`, bytes served through `serve` (`serveClip` from `@khalido/home/cast/serve`, passed to `cast()`). It reads the receiver status (and a Default Media Receiver's media status), for a group re-runs discovery first and reconnects if the leader moved, sets the volume if given and unmutes a muted target (as two requests: a `SET_VOLUME` takes a level or a mute, not both), launches the Default Media Receiver (`CC1AD845`), `LOAD`s the clip and waits for its media session to go `IDLE` with `FINISHED` (60 s plus the clip's length when the device reports it, or `timeoutMs`). Then, always: what the Default Media Receiver was playing is loaded again at its `currentTime`, an idle device gets `STOP`, then the volume and mute (a group's its own level first, then member by member, through each member's own socket, from the group's multizone status). Another app (Spotify, YouTube) is interrupted, as Google's own announcements do, and the result names it in `notRestored`. It returns `{ played, why?, restored, notRestored }` and throws only before anything changed (an unknown target, a socket down). A clip gets 15 s to reach `PLAYING`, then the name of its type goes in the failure, since a device that can't play it took 35-44 s to say so; a receiver error sooner carries its reason and code. `timeoutMs` replaces both waits. `announceAll(targets, clip, options)` does several at once, one result per device, an unreachable target its own `played: false`; a group and one of its members would clash, untried.
- `serveClip(bytes, type, { host?, signal? })` (`node:http`, Node-only like `tls.ts`): an address of this machine on the target's network (else the first non-internal IPv4), a random port and an unguessable path, `Content-Type` and `Content-Length`; the whole body to every GET and HEAD (a `Range` probe gets a full 200, which HTTP allows), so a player that probes before it fetches still gets the clip. It closes on `close()`, which `announce` calls once the clip has played, when `signal` aborts, or after 60 s. Not yet seen live: how the Default Media Receiver fetches, and the macOS local-network prompt the first serve may raise.
- CLI: `home cast announce --to <name> [--to <name>…] (--say "<text>" | --file <path>) [--volume 0-100]`, `home cast volume <name> <0-100>`, `home cast mute <name> on|off`, `home cast stop <name>`. `--say` runs `say -o <tmp>.m4a --file-format=m4af --data-format=aac` (without the data format, m4af holds uncompressed PCM), serves it as `audio/mp4` and deletes the file. The library never runs `say`.
- Config: one entry per device or group, `{ "connector": "cast", "name": "…", "castId": "…" }`, all folded into one `cast` integration; `home discover --write` fills them and refreshes a renamed entry's `name` (the same `castId` with a new `fn`), printed as `renamed`. No host: discovery gives every address.

## Protocol

CastV2 is TLS to port 8009 (a group: its own port), carrying length-prefixed protobuf `CastMessage`s (`cast_channel.proto`: version, source, destination, namespace, payload type, payload). Every namespace here sends JSON in `payload_utf8`, so `codec.ts` encodes and decodes that one message by hand (about 100 lines with framing and the channel type) rather than taking `protobufjs`. Namespaces: `tp.connection` (`CONNECT`, `CLOSE`), `tp.heartbeat` (`PING`, `PONG`), `receiver` (`GET_STATUS` → `RECEIVER_STATUS`), `media` (on an app's transport), `multizone` (`GET_STATUS` → `MULTIZONE_STATUS`, then `DEVICE_ADDED`, `DEVICE_UPDATED`, `DEVICE_REMOVED`).

**Live, KO's seven entries, 2026-10-13 (Bun 1.4.2, read-only).** Every one answered TLS plus both `GET_STATUS`es in 33–111 ms; TLS 1.2 on the Nest Mini and Home Speaker, 1.3 elsewhere. The recorded payloads, ids and names replaced, are in `test/fixtures/cast/`.

- **Certificates** are self-signed, valid 48 hours, so they rotate. The name (CN) is the device's id as a dashed UUID: the mDNS `id` with dashes.
- **Groups.** A group is its own mDNS entry, at its leader's address on a high port, and presents **its leader's certificate**. Multizone status on the group's port lists the members (by `deviceId`, the same dashed id) with their volumes; on a device's own port it lists only that device, so membership is read from group sockets only. TXT `rm` does not mark membership. `GET_CASTING_GROUPS` is refused.
- **Receiver status**: hubs and the Chromecast Ultra run Backdrop (`E8C28D3C`) with `isIdleScreen: true`; the speakers report no application; the TV Streamer reports `isStandBy: true`, `isActiveInput: false` and a `fixed` volume; the Ultra's volume is `attenuation`.
- No device sent a `PING` in 12 s of listening, so our 5 s ping is what keeps the socket's silence check honest.
- Media status first seen live 2026-11-09 (Bun 1.4.4): a paused title and artist on a TV.
- **First announcements, 2026-11-09, from a Pi on 0.1.1.** MP3 played on two audio-only speakers and two displays, 7-8 s each, the clip fetched from the Pi's LAN address, idle and volume restored. A 24 kHz mono WAV played on the displays and failed on both audio-only speakers, "the clip stopped: ERROR" after 35-44 s. One WAV format tried: use MP3 or AAC.
- **On 0.1.2, 2026-11-10, from the Pi (Bun 1.4.4; chota-bot's session, KO listening).** MP3 at volume 30 on one audio-only speaker: played and restored in 2 s. To a group of four: played in 9.5 s, each member back at its own level. `setVolume` and `setMuted` on one speaker read back as set. Over Spotify playing on the speaker: played, the volume restored, Spotify listed in `notRestored`, and KO had to pick the speaker again in the Spotify app.
- **A group's own level was not restored** (same run; fixed in 0.1.3, confirmed below): after the group announce at 30 the group read 30 (it had been 53) though every member was back; setting the group to 53 afterwards did not move the members. `restoreVolume` restored members only; fixed in 0.1.3: the group's own level goes back first, then each member's.
- **From a Mac, 2026-11-11 (Bun 1.4.4).** `home cast announce --to <a Nest Hub> --say … --volume 40`: played, idle and volume restored, 6 s in all; KO heard it.
- **On 0.1.3, 2026-11-11, from the Pi (Bun 1.4.4; chota-bot's session).** A group announce at volume 30: played, and the group and all four members read their earlier levels straight after and 10 s later. With Spotify cast to the group of four: every member read the group's track "via" the group at its own level, `castStatus` gave each member and the group `busy`, and `castFree` over every target left only the idle TV free. So what `castFree` assumes members report is what they report. `castFree` over every target at idle: the group and an idle TV free, a TV with Spotify paused skipped as busy, a device not in discovery skipped as unknown.
- Not seen yet: `stop`, an announce over a Default Media Receiver stream, `announceAll`, a leader change, member changes, and Node's `tls` (no Node caller yet).

## TLS: verification off, the name checked

As with [Enphase](../enphase/README.md#tls-why-the-bun-path-skips-verification), verification is off, here because there is nothing to verify against: each device signs its own certificate and rotates it every two days, and Google's device-auth challenge would need its Cast root. Instead `cast()` checks the certificate's name against discovery: a device's must be its own id, and a group's must be a known device's id, which is then its `leader`. A mismatch (an address now held by another device) closes the socket and discovers again. What this protects against: devices that have shuffled addresses, so one is never read as another. What it does not: a host on the LAN can mint a certificate with any name it likes, so it is not authentication. Nothing secret is sent on these sockets. A write goes to whichever device answers at an address whose certificate names the target, with the same caveat.

`tls.ts` (`node:tls`) and `serve.ts` (`node:http`) are the connector's Node APIs, so they sit outside the runtime-neutral build like `weather/ftp.ts` and are passed in (`connect`, `serve`).
